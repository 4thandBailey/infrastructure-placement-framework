# Module 5 — AI Governance and NIST Alignment

**Pillar:** Protect it fully · **Framework:** Infrastructure Placement Framework v1.0.0

---

## The question

Does your organization have a defensible, standards-based framework for how AI is used, what data enters AI systems, and who is accountable for AI decisions?

---

## Context

AI is now embedded in virtually every productivity tool employees use daily — Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Gemini, Salesforce Einstein, GitHub Copilot, and hundreds of other tools with AI features enabled by default. Most organizations have three distinct AI problems that require different responses.

**Shadow AI** — employees using unapproved tools without oversight — is present in virtually every organization. The question is not whether it is happening. It is what data has already been exposed and whether any of it creates regulatory liability.

---

## Three market segments, one framework

| Segment | Primary problem | Module response |
|---|---|---|
| Large enterprises | Protecting IP and proprietary data from LLM training | AI Data Governance Policy on NIST AI 600-1 |
| SMBs | Structured AI adoption from a standing start | 90-day AI Readiness Program on NIST AI RMF 1.0 |
| All organizations | Shadow AI audit and remediation | Shadow AI audit + data exposure assessment |

---

## Standards foundation

| Standard | Scope |
|---|---|
| NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023) | Govern · Map · Measure · Manage |
| NIST AI 600-1 Generative AI Profile (July 2024) | 12 generative AI risk categories |
| NIST CSF 2.0 (2024) | Govern · Identify · Protect · Detect · Respond · Recover |
| NIST IR 8596 Cyber AI Profile (December 2025) | AI-specific cybersecurity considerations |
